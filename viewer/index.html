Yelamu: an interactive 3D map of San Francisco around 1769

Yelamu is a free, interactive 3D map of the San Francisco peninsula as its land and water may have lain around 1769, before the city. The peninsula is the homeland of the Ramaytush, who are a living community today. The map is reconstructed from modern elevation data, from historical ecology maps and, along parts of the bay shore, from 1850s U.S. Coast Survey maps. It is an informed picture, not a photograph or a survey, and it is not for navigation, engineering or legal use.

Layers: terrain, water and habitat (bay, marsh, wetland, dunes, grassland, scrub, woodland), freshwater ponds, historical creeks, modelled stream channels, illustrative vegetation shapes, the general locations of five village sites named by SFEI (approximate, not official), modern streets, and 47 modern landmarks for orientation. Hotspots are cards about 18 landscape features such as Laguna Honda, Lake Merced, Mission Bay marsh and Yerba Buena Cove. Each layer and each card states its source and how sure we are: Documented, Inferred or Illustrative.

Data from the San Francisco Estuary Institute (Hidden Nature SF), USGS 3DEP, NOAA and the U.S. Census Bureau.
