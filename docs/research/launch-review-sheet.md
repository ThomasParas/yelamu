## About dialog (verbatim)

**What this map is.** This map shows the landscape of the San Francisco peninsula around 1769. It is reconstructed from modern elevation data and from historical ecology maps. It is an informed picture of how land and water may have lain, not a photograph or a survey.

**The Ramaytush Ohlone.** The San Francisco peninsula is the homeland of the Ramaytush Ohlone. The Ramaytush Ohlone are a living community today, not only a people of the past. To learn from them directly, visit the Association of Ramaytush Ohlone (link to ramaytush.org). This map shows land and water only. It does not depict villages, people or sacred sites.

**How sure are we.** Each layer and each hotspot says how well supported it is: *Documented*: Stated by a cited source. *Inferred*: Reconstructed by this project from other data. *Illustrative*: Drawn to give an impression, not a record.

**Credits.** San Francisco Estuary Institute. 2021. Hidden Nature SF, version 1.0 (hidden-nature.org). U.S. Geological Survey 3D Elevation Program. U.S. Census Bureau TIGER/Line. NOAA National Geodetic Survey, Shoreline Data Rescue Project (historical shoreline vectors, 1850s). three.js.

## Legend (verbatim)

GROUND: Bay, ocean and ponds. Tidal marsh and flats. Freshwater wetland and riparian. Beach. Dunes. Grassland. Scrub. Oak and bay woodland. Rock. Not yet reconstructed (modern terrain shown for context). *Ponds and lagoons sit at their own water level.*
MAP SYMBOLS: Hotspot: select it to read about the place. Text label: a landmark. Positions are approximate. Thin dark line: a modern street, for orientation only. *Vegetation shapes (trees, shrubs, reeds) are illustrative: they show the kind of plants, not individual plants.*

"Not yet reconstructed (modern terrain shown for context)" is the grey-brown ground in the south-west and parts of the south, where SFEI maps no habitat: a visitor will see a lot of it. Consider whether "not yet reconstructed" is the wording you want.
