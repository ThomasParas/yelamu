# Creeks results

Design: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-18-creeks-design.md`. Plan: `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-11-18-creeks.md`. Each section says what was **not** checked. Updated the same day (Task 4b) for the overlap rule below. Browser and benchmark results (Task 8) are in "Viewer and browser results" and "Benchmark A to E" below.

## What was drawn, and from what (2026-11-18)

`viewer/public/assets/real-city/creeks.json` holds two sets of lines in the viewer's world frame (metres from the terrain centre, x east, z south), written by `pipeline/scripts/build_creeks.py` from two public SFEI ArcGIS layers (downloaded by `fetch_creeks.py` into the git-ignored `pipeline/data/raw/sfei-creeks/`):

- **Hand-mapped creeks**: SFEI's "San Francisco Historical Creeks" (26 features, `san_francisco_historical_creeks.geojson`). All 26 survive the build, as 26 lines (none split, none lost). 482 points, 16.2 km. Confidence: 8 **documented** (SFEI's own "well located" flag) and 18 **inferred** (the other 17 and one marked "Sometimes dry"). Ten of the 26 carry a name, eight distinct names: Lobos Creek, Arroyo Dolores, Old Arroyo Dolores, Chutchui Creek, Ojo de Agua de Figueroa, El Polin, Coyote Gulch, and Petlenuc Creek, which is three separate lines (`creek-17`, `creek-18`, `creek-19`).
- **Modelled streams**: SFEI's "MYHL_HistoricStreams" (`myhl_historic_streams_order3.geojson`: 5,184 features of Strahler order 3 and up, 2,803 of order 3, 1,765 of order 4, 586 of order 5, 30 of order 6; one order-3 feature is a MultiLineString of two lines, so the build reads 5,185 line segments, 2,804 of order 3). The file covers the whole city and more (lat 37.590 to 37.805, lon -122.514 to -122.380), not only the south. Segments that meet end to end with the same order are merged into channels. Modelled pieces within **100 m of a hand-mapped creek are not drawn** (the overlap rule, below). Result: **225 channels** (133 of order 3, 64 of order 4, 25 of order 5, 3 of order 6), 2,421 points, 132.1 km. Illustrative: computed channels, not observed creeks.

**The overlap rule (owner decision, 2026-11-18).** The first build (Task 4) drew the modelled channels everywhere, which put about 13 km of modelled line on top of hand-mapped creeks (see answer 3). The owner decided each creek is drawn once, by the hand-mapped line. `build_creeks.py` now cuts every trimmed modelled segment against the union of the hand-mapped lines (as drawn, after trimming and simplifying) buffered by 100 m (`--overlap-buffer`, default 100, 0 turns it off; recorded as `processing.overlap_buffer_m`) and keeps only the parts outside, before the segments are chained. It removed 15,085 m of modelled segment length (the `dropped_near_mapped_m` statistic, measured before simplification). The hand-mapped lines are identical to the first build (26 lines, 482 points); only the modelled set changed.

| | before (no overlap rule) | after (100 m) |
|---|---|---|
| Modelled channels | 247 (145 / 70 / 29 / 3 of order 3 / 4 / 5 / 6) | 225 (133 / 64 / 25 / 3) |
| Modelled length | 147.2 km | 132.1 km (order 3: 71.2, 4: 41.3, 5: 18.2, 6: 1.4) |
| Modelled points | 2,704 | 2,421 |
| Points in the file | 3,186 | 2,903 |
| `creeks.json` | 55,465 bytes | 51,004 bytes |

After the rule, 0.4 m of modelled line lies inside the 100 m buffer (a rounding effect of simplification; none inside 95 m), and the nearest any hand-mapped line end comes to a remaining modelled line is 99.9 m.

Processing: simplified at 3 m (the default), pieces shorter than 30 m after trimming dropped, lines cut where they enter open bay or ocean in this project's 1769 habitat raster (classes "Open bay (inferred from modern elevation)", "Bay (hand-drawn)", "Deep ocean/bay", "Shallow ocean/bay"; ponds, lagoons, marsh and tidal flat are not cut). A point outside the 1771 x 1565 grid counts as wet.

`creeks.json` is **51,004 bytes (51 KB)** with **2,903 points** in total (482 mapped + 2,421 modelled); the first build, before the overlap rule, was 55,465 bytes and 3,186 points. That is far under the 1.5 MB threshold in the plan, so the default 3 m tolerance stays and `--tolerance 5` was not needed or run.

`meta.json` changed only by `creeks_file`, `creeks_mapped` (26), `creeks_modelled` (225) and two `hand_sources` rows (`creeks`, confidence inferred, count 26; `modelled_streams`, confidence illustrative, count 225). Every existing key and row, including `shade` and `overlay_villages`, is untouched. The overlap rule changed only the `modelled_streams` row (its text now ends "Strahler order 3 and up, left out within 100 m of a hand-mapped creek. Computed, not observed." and its count went from 247 to 225) and `creeks_modelled`. The two row texts are proposed Sources wording and still await the owner's approval (Task 7).

## Answers to the six questions

1. **Points and size.** 3,186 points, 55,465 bytes; tolerance 3 m. See above.
2. **Modelled channels.** After the overlap rule: 225, by order: 133 (3), 64 (4), 25 (5), 3 (6). Length by order: 71.2 km, 41.3 km, 18.2 km, 1.4 km. Merging took 5,185 line segments down to channels (a confluence or a change of order keeps segments apart). Before the rule: 247 channels (145, 70, 29, 3), 78.7 km, 46.4 km, 20.7 km and 1.4 km.
3. **The lat 37.749 to 37.755 gap: there was none, and the premise was wrong; the overlap was resolved by the owner's rule.** The modelled file that was downloaded is not limited to the south of the city. Its order-3-and-up lines run from lat 37.590 (south of the city rectangle) to lat 37.805, so the modelled set also covers the area the hand-mapped set covers. The facts below were measured on the first build, before the overlap rule:
   - The hand-mapped layer ends at lat 37.7552 (`creek-1`, south end at world (686.5, 1633.4)), 13.3 m from the nearest modelled line. 21 of the 26 hand-mapped lines touch a modelled line (0 m, or 0.3 m for `creek-10`) or come within 5 m of one (`creek-16`, 4.9 m).
   - 16 modelled channels (11 of order 3, 4 of order 4, 1 of order 5) have a point inside the lat 37.749 to 37.755 band; no hand-mapped line does. So south of lat 37.755 only modelled lines are drawn, and there is no visible break between the two sets.
   - **Overlap north of lat 37.755.** 68.8 km of the 147.2 km of modelled output lies north of lat 37.755, where the hand-mapped lines are. 13.4 km of modelled output runs within 50 m of a hand-mapped line (15.1 km within 100 m). In those places the same creek would be drawn twice (the hand-mapped line over the modelled one). It also means the draft Legend text for "Modelled streams" ("for the south of the city, where no hand-mapped creeks exist yet") is not accurate as written. The owner decided to hide modelled channels within 100 m of a hand-mapped line (see "The overlap rule" above), which also makes the Legend wording "for the south of the city" unnecessary: the proposed text is now "shown where no hand-mapped creek exists".
   - **Consequence of the rule at the south end of the hand-mapped layer.** The 900 m order-3 modelled channel that followed `creek-1` (13.3 m from its south end) lay wholly inside the 100 m buffer and is gone, so `creek-1`'s south end at (686.5, 1633.4) is now 675.7 m from the nearest drawn modelled line (an order-3 channel at x 1,344 to 1,750, z 1,347 to 1,790). The visible hand-over between the two sets that existed in the first build is now a gap of that size at `creek-1`. The hand-mapped line ends where SFEI ends it; whether the creek really continued south as the modelled channel suggests was not checked. Other hand-mapped ends are at least 99.9 m from a drawn modelled line, and the farthest (`creek-22`, `creek-23`, `creek-21`) 1.5 to 1.9 km.
4. **Mission Creek mouth.** Lon -122.39, lat 37.77 is world (3514, -22), in the cell class "Bay (hand-drawn)", 5 m from the nearest bay-mask cell. No hand-mapped line lies within 200 m of it: the nearest is Chutchui Creek at 2,234 m (then `creek-1` at 2,784 m and `creek-4` at 2,892 m). The nearest modelled line is 1,086 m away (first build; the overlap rule can only make it farther). The hand-mapped layer does reach this project's shore at Mission Bay, further west: Chutchui Creek ends 13 m from the bay mask in Tidal marsh at (1288, 165), Arroyo Dolores 7 m from it at (784, 956) and `creek-1` 10 m at (894, 950). So the lower reach of Mission Creek between roughly x = 1,300 and the mouth point is not drawn by either set.
5. **Length removed by the bay trim.** Hand-mapped: 17,035 m raw, 16,394 m after trimming, so 641 m (3.8 percent) removed; simplification then took another 150 m, leaving 16,244 m. The trim is concentrated in Arroyo Dolores (2,406 to 2,001 m, 17 percent), `creek-22` (146 to 94 m) and `creek-23` (140 to 81 m); the other lines lose a few percent at most. Modelled (order 3 and up): 278,766 m raw, 147,539 m after trimming, so 131,228 m (47.1 percent) removed; chaining, simplification and dropping channels shorter than 30 m took another 337 m, leaving 147,202 m. These are the first-build figures. With the overlap rule, a further 15,085 m of trimmed modelled segments is removed before chaining and 132,099 m is drawn. **Nearly all of that trim is not bay**: 129,594 m (46.5 percent of the raw length) lies outside the city rectangle (the file runs south to lat 37.59, beyond the rectangle's south edge at lat 37.700), and only 763 m (0.3 percent) falls in the bay mask. The rest, 148,409 m (53.2 percent), is dry land inside the rectangle.

## What was not checked (when the data was built; the viewer bullets are answered further down)

- **Where the lines sit in the viewer.** Everything above is computed in the world frame against the habitat raster. Nothing was loaded in a browser; whether the lines follow the terrain valleys, how they meet the shore and the ponds, and how they look at the opening zoom come with the viewer tasks. (Answered in "Viewer and browser results" below.)
- **The accuracy of SFEI's lines.** The hand-mapped lines were not compared with the 19th-century maps they cite; the modelled lines are SFEI's computation from a historical elevation model and were not checked against anything. One check was made: the Chutchui Creek and village cross-check in the design (an agreement of two readings of SFEI's own work, not an independent measurement).
- **The preview image** (a flat plan view over the habitat raster, made for this task and not committed) was looked at once; the lines were not compared with SFEI's own map.
- **Whether the modelled lines the overlap rule removed are the same creeks** as the hand-mapped ones. Only the distance between them was measured (13.4 km within 50 m, 15.1 km within 100 m, as above); a modelled channel 80 m from a hand-mapped line could be a different tributary. The 100 m figure is the owner's choice and was not tuned against any map.
- **The look of the result.** The overlap rule and the new gap at `creek-1` were not seen in a browser or on the preview image, and the preview image was not redrawn after the rule. (The gap at `creek-1` was seen in a browser later; see below.)
- **The viewer.** Nothing in the viewer reads `creeks.json` yet, so the changed counts are not reflected in any Legend, Sources or toggle behaviour; those come with the viewer tasks. (Done; see below.)
- **The licence.** SFEI's layers carry no stated licence for either file; the owner's request to SFEI is outstanding. The modelled layer's description has unfilled blanks.
- **One small difference from the design's numbers, and one resolved.** The design counts 8 named hand-mapped features; the file holds 10 named features (eight names). It was not checked whether SFEI changed the layer since the design was written or the design miscounted. The modelled count is not a difference: the file holds 5,184 features (2,803 of order 3, as the design says), one of which is a MultiLineString of two lines, which is why the build counts 5,185 line segments (2,804 of order 3). The design's modelled extent (lat 37.692 to 37.749) came from the item page and was wrong or stale; the downloaded file spans lat 37.590 to 37.805 and lon -122.514 to -122.380.

## Viewer and browser results (2026-11-18)

Checked in a real browser (Playwright, desktop 1400 x 900 at 1x pixel ratio, and phone width 390 x 844 by window resize) against the production build (`npm run build`, `vite preview`). Screenshots are in `.playwright-mcp/` (git-ignored): `creeks-01-opening-desktop.png`, `creeks-02-mission-zoom4.png`, `creeks-03-chutchui-zoom12.png`, `creeks-04-lobos-mouth-baker-beach.png`, `creeks-05-petlenuc-crissy-field-mouth.png`, `creeks-06-islais-creek-mouth.png`, `creeks-07-hills-closeup.png`, `creeks-08-legend-light.png`, `creeks-09-sources.png`, `creeks-10-legend-dark.png`, `creeks-11-creek1-south-end.png`, `creeks-12-phone-opening.png` to `creeks-16-phone-legend.png`.

- **The first build drew nothing you could see at the opening view. Fixed by widening the ribbons (`216fa64`).** At the opening zoom the city is about 38 px per km across, so the 12 m hand-mapped and 6, 9 and 12 m modelled ribbons were 0.3 to 0.5 px wide: with both toggles on, no creek line showed at all, although the layer was present and visible in the scene. Widths tried: 30 m mapped with 15, 20 and 26 m modelled (clear at the opening view, but about 14 px wide at zoom 12, like a river); 22 m with 11, 15 and 19 m (only just visible at the opening view); **final: `CREEK_WIDTH_M` 26 and `MODELLED_WIDTH_M` 13, 18 and 22** (order 3, 4, 5 and up). 26 m is about one pixel at the opening view, about 4 px at zoom 4 and 10 to 11 px at zoom 12, which is heavy next to a real creek (a few metres) but readable and consistent with the village discs. This is a compromise, not an ideal: one world width cannot be thin when close and visible when far. Only the width constants (and their comment) changed. Opening view at the final widths: a faint, fine network of lines over the dunes and the grey hills; the hand-mapped lines cannot be told from the modelled ones at that zoom (the difference in colour strength and width shows from about zoom 4). On a phone at its opening zoom (the whole region is about a third of the screen) no line is visible; fine lines appear from about zoom 5.
- **Mission area (Arroyo Dolores, Chutchui Creek, Sitlintac).** The lines run along the terrain valleys on the grey hills and across the flat; Arroyo Dolores and Chutchui Creek come down into the Mission Creek inlet and stop at the shallows. Village labels are HTML on top of the canvas, so a ribbon cannot hide a name, and "Chutchui", "Sitlintac", "Mission Dolores" and "Dolores Park" all stayed readable at zoom 4 and 12. The ribbons are drawn over the village discs (render order 1.6 against 1.2 and 1.4): at Chutchui two hand-mapped creeks cross the disc and at Petlenuc a three-branched Petlenuc Creek crosses the whole disc, and each covers a strip of the fill. The disc and its ring stay readable, though at zoom 12 the ring looked to run over the ribbon at one crossing (not looked at further).
- **Creek mouths.** Lobos Creek at Baker Beach: the hand-mapped line ends at the sand edge, just above the foam, neither hidden nor in the bay. A thin modelled channel 100 m to the east ends in a straight stub on the same beach. Petlenuc Creek at Crissy Field: two branches reach the shore and end at the foam, none enters the water. **Islais Creek: no creek line is drawn for it.** The nearest drawn line to the Islais Creek estuary hotspot is a 104 m order-3 stub 222 m away, and the other order-3 pieces within 1.5 km are 45 to 263 m long (short stubs on the shore cliffs; the longest nearby line is an 839 m order-4 channel 1.5 km away); no channel of order 3 and up crosses the estuary in SFEI's modelled set. So the one named creek mouth in the south shows nothing; the Precita Creek mouth to the north is drawn.
- **`creek-1`, the south end near world (686, 1633).** After the overlap rule the line stops square-cut on the grey hillside (the north side of Bernal Heights, not at water) and nothing carries on: the nearest drawn modelled line is 676 m away (about 90 px at zoom 6). It reads as a creek that simply ends, not as a break between two sets. That is not wrong, but the end is visible, and the viewer cannot tell that SFEI's modelled channel, which would have continued it, was dropped on purpose. Other hand-mapped lines also end square-cut (for example north-west of Corona Heights); that is how SFEI's lines end.
- **Hills and floating.** The ribbons follow the slope at every zoom looked at, up to zoom 14 on the steep hills west of the Mission, and did not look raised (lift 3 m). The camera only turns about the vertical axis (tilt is fixed), so a side-on look, where floating would show most, could not be taken.
- **Contrast, by eye.** The dark blue is strong on sand, grassland, the pale grey and brown hills and the village discs; weaker, but readable, on dark oak woodland and on shaded slopes, where the 0.7-opacity modelled lines are the faintest. Not measured beyond `uiColors.test.ts`.
- **Toggles and links.** Both toggles ("Historical creeks", "Modelled streams") come right after "Village sites", both on by default, labelled checkboxes in the "Layers" group of the accessibility tree. Turning one off hides only its group (the scene graph showed `creeks-mapped` and `creeks-modelled` flip separately; the map was not compared pixel by pixel). The ids in the URL hash are `creeks` and `modelled-streams`, for example `l=creeks,hotspots,landmarks,modelled-streams,vegetation,villages,water-motion` with everything on after a first toggle; the hash carries no `l=` until the visitor toggles something. Loading a URL whose `l=` lacks `creeks` keeps the hand-mapped lines off and the modelled ones on. **An old share link `#l=streets,hotspots` loaded fresh shows both creek layers off** (and Vegetation, Water motion, Landmarks and Village sites off too, as any explicit list does); `#` with no `l` shows both on.
- **Legend and Sources.** The two Legend rows have swatches (a dark pill for the creeks, a lighter pill for the modelled streams) and the proposed wording, in light and dark mode (in dark mode the swatch sits on a light chip, as the village and street swatches do) and at phone width. The Sources panel has "Historical creeks" (Inferred, 26 lines) and "Modelled streams" (Illustrative, 225 channels) after "Village sites".
- **Console and accessibility tree.** No errors or warnings on any load (only the existing "shore field" info line). The checkbox names are as expected.
- **Opening load.** In this headless Chrome the first full screen took 10 to 25 s to appear and the surrounding terrain streamed in after the city; not compared with a build without creeks.

(The `?debug` page reports different totals for scenario A, about 867,000 without the creeks and 902,000 with; the figures above are from `?bench`.)
- **The creek layer's own size.** Read from the built meshes: **17,340 triangles**, of which 15,284 are the modelled streams (8,214 + 4,790 + 2,280 in three width bands) and 2,056 the hand-mapped creeks, with 17,842 vertices. That is about 26 percent of the 60,000 budget for the modelled part and about 26 percent of the 8,000 budget for the hand-mapped part. The renderer counts **34,680 triangles and 8 draw calls** for it, double, because the ribbons are transparent and double-sided, and three.js draws such a mesh twice (back faces, then front faces).
- **No tuning needed.** 60 fps and the budget were both met, so `--tolerance` was not raised (3 m stays) and `CREEK_STEP_M` stays 20.
- **Possible saving, not done.** The ribbons wind so that their front face looks down (worked out by hand, not tested), so a single-sided material would need the index order swapped; that would halve the cost (17,340 triangles, 4 calls). Not changed, because 60 fps is met and a phone has not been measured.
- **Tests:** viewer 808 passed (2 skipped), `tsc` clean, `vite build` clean. No pipeline code changed in this task.

**Not checked:** a phone run of `?bench` (the layer adds 8 draw calls and 34,680 triangles per frame, and a phone has not been asked to draw them); reduced motion (the layer is static and nothing in it is animated, but the setting was not switched on); an automated accessibility scan (axe), only the accessibility tree was read; a 2x pixel-ratio screen (everything was seen at 1x, where 26 m is one pixel; at 2x the same lines are two device pixels); Safari and Firefox; the dark-mode map itself (only the dark-mode Legend was looked at, with the map behind it); any rotated view other than one look at 0.6 radians; the overlap rule along the other 25 hand-mapped creeks, which were not walked one by one for gaps (only `creek-1`, the Mission, Lobos, Petlenuc and Islais areas were looked at); whether the faint opening-view lines are noticed by a visitor who does not know to look for them; the ring-over-ribbon crossing at the village discs; and whether toggling a layer off changes exactly its own pixels. The lines are not clickable and have no cards yet (Task 9).

## Removing the layer

Do all four steps; the first alone leaves the seven creek cards and the credit behind.

1. From `pipeline/`: `uv run python scripts/build_creeks.py ../viewer/public/assets/real-city --remove`. It deletes `viewer/public/assets/real-city/creeks.json` and removes `creeks_file`, `creeks_mapped`, `creeks_modelled` and the two `hand_sources` rows (`creeks`, `modelled_streams`) from `meta.json`. The viewer's Legend rows, toggles and Sources rows appear only when the dataset has creeks, so they go too.
2. Delete the SFEI creeks row from `NOTICE.md`.
3. Delete the seven named creek entries from `pipeline/data/overlay/hotspots.json` (ids `creek-arroyo-dolores`, `creek-old-arroyo-dolores`, `creek-chutchui`, `creek-petlenuc`, `creek-ojo-de-agua-de-figueroa`, `creek-el-polin`, `creek-coyote-gulch`; leave Lobos Creek, which was there before). Also remove or update the two tests in `pipeline/tests/test_overlay.py` that expect these seven cards in the shipped `hotspots.json` and `overlay.json`, and `pipeline/tests/test_creeks_shipped.py`, which expects a shipped `creeks.json`, or they will fail.
4. From `pipeline/`, rerun `uv run python scripts/build_overlay.py --region regions/full-city.json` (it needs the raw TIGER files in `pipeline/data/raw/tiger/`). This rewrites `overlay.json` without the seven hotspots.

`build_shade.py` and `build_creeks.py` do not need to be rerun after `--remove`. `build_overlay.py` does not drop the `shade` key: `patch_overlay_meta` in `pipeline/yelamu/overlay.py` copies `meta.json` and changes only `overlay_file`, `overlay_villages` and the `villages` row of `hand_sources`, keeping every other key and row (read in the code; the full removal, steps 1 to 4 together, was not run). After a city rebuild (not a removal) the README order applies: `build_overlay.py`, `build_shade.py`, `build_creeks.py`.

## Known limits

- **Islais Creek has a hotspot but no line.** No modelled channel of order 3 or higher crosses the Islais estuary in SFEI's set (see "Viewer and browser results").
- **`creek-1` ends on a hillside.** Its modelled continuation lay inside the 100 m overlap buffer and was dropped, so the line stops square-cut on the north side of Bernal Heights, 676 m from the nearest drawn modelled line.
- **Mission Creek's lower reach is not drawn.** Neither SFEI set covers it (answer 4 above).
- **The ribbons are drawn twice.** They are transparent and double-sided, so three.js draws each mesh twice (34,680 triangles and 8 draw calls counted for 17,340 triangles of geometry). A front-face-only material, with the index order swapped, would halve the cost; this was not done, as 60 fps was met.
- **Widths are in world metres.** The lines are faint at the opening zoom (about one pixel) and heavy up close (10 to 11 px at zoom 12), and on a phone at its opening zoom none shows.
- **Old share links with an explicit `l=` list hide the creek layers**, as they do the village sites and other newer layers; a link with no `l=` shows both on.
- **The licence is not yet confirmed in writing** for the two creek layers; the NOTICE row says so.
